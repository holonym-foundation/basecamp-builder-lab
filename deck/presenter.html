00:00–06:00 · Set up the problem

Open slide ↗

SHOW slides 1–6. Finish the architecture map by 06:00.

Talk track

“Muse, Instinct and Dots show the personal-agent experience people want. OpenClaw and Hermes make running and extending an agent something builders can do. But the runtime is only part of the job: where does it run, who pays, what can it access, and who can stop it?”

Bridge to AEX

“AEX brings those questions into one workflow: find resources, agree terms, fund an escrow, identify the agent and inspect its work. Identity and reputation need evidence. Sovereignty means retaining control and a meaningful choice of provider. The security plane governs actions outside the prompt.”

Set the promise

“We will change a recipe, inspect a real result and check evidence on Sui. The slides are signposts; most of this session happens in the browser.” Describe architecture as direction where it is not demonstrated. Do not claim every product named is integrated with AEX or that all compute settlement occurs on Sui.

Recovery card

Open slide ↗

Spend no more than 45 seconds recovering a live step.

AEX access or checkout fails

Keep the existing request/receipt. Move to browser Guard; do not pay again or share a company login. A code is admission, not proof that the backend is ready.

Guard or explorer network fails

Use the saved Guard baseline/edited logs from rehearsal, clearly labeled previous live reads. If an actual captured receipt exists, show that with its date. Otherwise use Hunt’s source cards and a recipe-writing exercise. Do not fabricate a digest or call offline output live.

Hunt model or browsing fails

Read the official-source cards already open in the Hunt page. Ask attendees to write the task, inputs, permissions and a verifiable result. A non-chain backup stays useful without pretending inference worked.

A transfer times out

Preserve the operation/digest. Check its existing outcome; never resend as a blind retry. Continue the pool-transaction evidence path.
